Giving is the most closely held information the church keeps. Only two roles
touch it, and the site enforces that: it is not a matter of trust or of
remembering not to look.

## The Counter

A Counter helps count an offering on Sunday. They can:

- start a count and enter the gifts in a count that is open;
- search givers by name, and add a new giver by name when someone is not on the list;
- choose a category for a designated gift, by name.

While a count is open, a Counter sees everything in that count: every giver
name, every amount and the totals for it. That is the nature of counting an
offering with two people at the table.

A Counter cannot see closed counts, anyone's giving history, totals by giver,
addresses, notes or giving statements. They have no year to choose and no
Stewardship tabs. Once a count is closed, it is out of their hands.

## The Treasurer

The Treasurer keeps the church's books. On top of everything a Counter can do,
the Treasurer can:

- close and reopen counts;
- see every count, every gift and every giver's history;
- keep giving records: names, mailing addresses and email addresses;
- link a closed count to the bank deposit (this also needs permission to keep
  the budget, which the Treasurer has);
- prepare, print and email year-end giving statements.

## Everybody else

Nobody else has any access to giving: not the pastor, not the deacons, not the
finance committee, and not administrators. An administrator can create the
Treasurer's account and give out the role, but cannot see a single gift.

The finance committee and the Treasurer do see the church's budget and
accounts, which show what was given in total, by category. They never show who
gave.

## What the audit log records

Every change is recorded with who made it and when. For giving, the entries name
the giving record and give counts, such as "12 sent, 1 failed". They never
contain a name of a giver, an amount or an email address, so the audit log
cannot become a second copy of the giving history.
